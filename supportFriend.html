    • Support a Friend Through Addiction

    Practical guidance to help a loved one on their recovery journey

    When someone you care about is struggling with addiction, knowing how to help can be challenging. This guide provides evidence-based strategies to support your friend while maintaining healthy boundaries and your own wellbeing.

    Remember: You can't force someone to recover, but you can be a source of strength, compassion, and practical support throughout their journey.

    Starting the Conversation

    How to approach your friend

    • Choose a private, calm setting when they're sober
    • Use "I" statements to express concern without blame
    • Focus on specific behaviors rather than labels
    • Listen more than you speak
    • Offer support rather than ultimatums
    "Instead of 'You have a problem,' try 'I've noticed you've been missing work lately and I'm concerned about you.'"
    Providing Practical Support

    Ways to assist in recovery

    • Offer to attend support meetings with them
    • Help research treatment options
    • Provide transportation to therapy sessions
    • Create a sober-friendly environment when together
    • Celebrate milestones and progress
    "Recovery isn't linear. Celebrate small victories along the way."
    Setting Boundaries

    Protecting yourself while helping

    • Clearly state what behaviors you won't tolerate
    • Don't enable by covering up consequences
    • Protect your finances and living space
    • Know when to step back for your own wellbeing
    • Seek support for yourself
    "Setting boundaries isn't rejection - it's creating a healthier relationship."
    Emotional Support

    Being there without enabling

    • Validate their feelings without validating harmful behavior
    • Be patient with the recovery process
    • Learn about addiction to understand their struggle
    • Focus on the person, not the addiction
    • Encourage healthy coping mechanisms
    "Addiction is a disease, not a moral failing. Compassion is key."
    Dealing with Relapse

    Responding to setbacks

    • Recognize relapse as part of recovery for many
    • Respond with concern, not anger or disappointment
    • Help them identify triggers and warning signs
    • Encourage returning to treatment immediately
    • Remind them of past successes
    "A relapse doesn't erase progress. It's an opportunity to strengthen recovery."
    Supporting Yourself

    Your wellbeing matters too

    • Join a support group for friends/family
    • Practice self-care regularly
    • Set aside time for your own life and interests
    • Recognize what you can and can't control
    • Seek professional help if needed
    "You can't pour from an empty cup. Caring for yourself helps you care for others."

    The Conversation Guide

    1

    Prepare for the Conversation

    Research addiction and treatment options beforehand. Plan what you want to say, focusing on specific concerns. Choose a time when your friend is sober and not stressed. Prepare yourself emotionally for various reactions.

    2

    Start with Compassion

    Begin with expressions of care: "I care about you and I'm concerned because..." Share specific observations without judgment: "I've noticed you've been missing our weekly dinners lately."

    3

    Listen Actively

    Allow them to respond without interruption. Validate their feelings: "I understand this might be difficult to talk about." Avoid debating or arguing about their substance use.

    4

    Offer Support

    Ask how you can help: "What would be most helpful to you right now?" Suggest specific resources: "Would you like me to find some local support groups?" Make it clear you're there for the long haul.

    DO:

    • Express concern with compassion
    • Focus on specific behaviors
    • Offer to help find resources
    • Set clear boundaries
    • Follow through on promises

    DON'T:

    • Lecture or moralize
    • Use labels like "addict" or "alcoholic"
    • Make threats you won't follow through on
    • Enable by covering up consequences
    • Take their behavior personally

    Practice Your Response

    What would you say in this situation? Choose the most supportive response:

    Your friend, who has been struggling with alcohol addiction, cancels plans with you for the third time this month. When you call to check on them, they sound intoxicated and say, "I'm fine, just tired. I don't need your lectures."

    Al-Anon Family Groups: Support for families of alcoholics
